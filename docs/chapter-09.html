Probability (PROB)

What is Probability?

Probability controls how often a drum sound actually plays when you tell it to. Instead of triggering every time, you can make sounds play randomly or occasionally.

Think of it like:

  • 100% chance = the sound plays every time (guaranteed)
  • 50% chance = the sound plays about half the time, randomly
  • 25% chance = the sound plays about one in four times
  • 0% chance = the sound never plays (silent)

The randomness creates **unpredictability and variation** in your patterns—the hallmark of living, breathing music.

Why Does Probability Matter?

Probability adds human feel and spontaneity to your patterns:

  • Create subtle movement: Add ghost notes that sometimes appear, sometimes don't
  • Add fills and breakups: Build tension with occasional breaks in the pattern
  • Realistic drumming: Real drummers don't hit with mechanical precision—sometimes they add extra hits, sometimes they skip them
  • Surprise and interest: Keep listeners engaged with unexpected variations
  • DJ-style tricks: Use probability to randomly drop elements for live mashup effects

Understanding Probability Values

Probability Value What Happens Musical Use
100% Plays every time (guaranteed) Main beats, core pattern
75% Plays about 3 out of 4 times Solid but with occasional drops
50% Plays about half the time (random) Fills, hi-hat variations, embellishments
25% Plays about 1 out of 4 times Rare ghost notes, spice hits
0% Never plays (silent) Disabled step; won't sound

How to Adjust Probability

  1. Select the track you want to edit on the main screen
  2. Short Press the encoder to enter track-edit mode
  3. Rotate the encoder until you are on the step you want to edit
  4. Medium Press the encoder to enter Step Edit mode
  5. Rotate the encoder to cycle through the popup items until you see "PROBABILITY"
  6. Short Press encoder (cursor moves to the value field)
  7. Rotate the encoder to change the probability value (0–100%)
  8. Lower values = plays less often. Higher values = plays more often
  9. Short press encoder to accept selected value
  10. Short press KEY0 to exit step edit mode and return to track-edit mode

Using the Edit Popup

When you press medium press, a popup appears showing all editable parameters for the selected step:

Edit Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        +00
    DECAY        50%
   >PROBABILITY< 100%
    MUTE   OFF
    CHAIN   NO
  1. Navigate between parameters: Rotate the encoder to scroll through Velocity, Pitch, Decay, Probability, Mute, and Chain. The highlighted parameter is between `>..<`.
  2. Edit a parameter: When a parameter is highlighted, press the encoder (short press) to enter edit mode
  3. Change the value: Rotate the encoder to increase or decrease the value: 
    Edit Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        +00
    DECAY        50%
    PROBABILITY  >50%<
    MUTE   OFF
    CHAIN   NO
  4. Confirm the value: Press the encoder (short press) to lock in the new value and return to parameter selection
  5. Continue editing: Repeat steps 1-4 to adjust other parameters
  6. Close the popup: Press KEY0 (short press) to close the popup. Your changes are saved.

Practical Examples

Adding Ghost Notes to a Snare

Create realistic snare patterns with occasional ghost notes:

Step:        1  2  3  4
Probability: 100 50 100 25

Step 1 always plays, step 2 plays half the time (creates roll effect), step 3 always plays, step 4 rarely plays (spice). Every loop sounds a bit different.

Creating Hi-Hat Variation

Make hi-hats feel natural and human, not robotic:

Step:        1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
Probability: 90 80 95 70 85 100 75 80

Most steps play (70–100%), but each has a slightly different chance. The hat feels alive and natural.

Building Surprise Fills

Use low probability on occasional steps to create breakdown and fill effects:

Step:        1  2  3  4
Probability: 100 100 100 30

3 out of 4 times, the pattern repeats normally. 1 out of 4 times, step 4 adds an extra element (a crash or fill). Your audience never knows when it's coming.

Pro Tips

One Loop, Many Shows: Because probability is random, each playthrough is slightly different. The same pattern never sounds quite the same way twice—perfect for live performance.

Ghost Notes: Set low probability (10–30%) on extra "ghost notes" (weak taps) between main beats. This adds texture without overwhelming the groove.

Fills and Breakups: Place a 50% probability element on an extra kick or snare hit to randomly create modern "glitch" or "trap" flavor without being predictable.

Combine with Velocity: Low-probability ghost notes should also have low velocity, so they're quiet when they appear and don't surprise with loudness.

Don't Overuse: If everything has 50% probability, nothing stands out and the groove becomes chaotic. Use probability sparingly—keep your core beat at 100% and add variation on top.

Important Note: Randomness

Probability uses random chance every time you loop your pattern. This means:

  • Each playthrough is unique
  • You might hear a step play several times in a row by coincidence, or not at all
  • This is musically interesting for listening, but for precise performances, keep important beats at 100%

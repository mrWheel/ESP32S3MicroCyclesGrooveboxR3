Pitch (PITCH)

What is Pitch?

Pitch controls how high or low a drum sound plays. It's the musical tone or frequency of the sound.

Think of how:

  • A kick drum can be deep and low or punchy and higher
  • A tom drum can be low (floor tom), mid (mid tom), or high (tom)
  • Your voice changes pitch when you speak higher or lower

On the Groovebox, pitch lets you shift any drum sound up or down by up to 12 semitones in either direction. That's one full octave of musical range.

Why Does Pitch Matter?

Adjusting pitch adds variation and musicality to your patterns:

  • Create melodies: Use pitch to make drum fills that sound like they're singing or talking
  • Add movement: Gradually shift pitch across steps to create ascending or descending patterns
  • Sound design: Completely transform a drum sound by shifting its pitch
  • Simulate different drums: One sample can behave like a low tom, mid tom, or high tom by changing the pitch

Understanding Pitch Values

Pitch is measured in semitones—the musical steps you'd find on a piano or guitar. You can shift sounds up to two full octaves in either direction:

Pitch Value What You Hear Example Use
-24 Extremely low (two octaves down) Ultra-deep, sub-bass-like sound
-12 Much lower (one full octave down) Deep, boom-like sound
-6 Noticeably lower Floor tom-like tone
0 Original, unmodified pitch Natural drum sound
+6 Noticeably higher High tom-like tone
+12 Much higher (one full octave up) Bright, thin sound
+24 Extremely high (two octaves up) Thin, squeaky, bell-like sound

How to Adjust Pitch

  1. Select the track you want to edit on the main screen
  2. Short Press the encoder to enter track-edit mode
  3. Rotate the encoder until you are on the step you want to edit
  4. Medium Press the encoder to enter Step Edit mode
  5. Rotate the encoder to cycle through the popup items until you see "PITCH"
  6. Short Press encoder (cursor moves to the value field)
  7. Rotate the encoder to change the pitch value (-24 to +24 semitones)
  8. Lower values = lower pitch. Higher values = higher pitch
  9. Short press encoder to accept selected value
  10. Short press KEY0 to exit step edit mode and return to track-edit mode

Using the Step Popup

When you press medium press, a popup appears showing all editable parameters for the selected step:

Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
   >PITCH<       +00
    DECAY        50%
    PROBABILITY  100%
    STEP   ON
    CHAIN   NO
  1. Navigate between parameters: Rotate the encoder to scroll through Velocity, Pitch, Decay, Probability, Step On/Off, and Chain. The highlighted parameter is between `>..<`.
  2. Edit a parameter: When a parameter is highlighted, press the encoder (short press) to enter edit mode
  3. Change the value: Rotate the encoder to increase or decrease the value: 
    Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        >+06<
    DECAY        50%
    PROBABILITY  100%
    STEP   ON
    CHAIN   NO
  4. Confirm the value: Press the encoder (short press) to lock in the new value and return to parameter selection
  5. Continue editing: Repeat steps 1-4 to adjust other parameters
  6. Close the popup: Press KEY0 (short press) to close the popup. Your changes are saved.

Practical Examples

Creating a Melodic Fill

Use one drum sample on different pitch values to create a descending fill:

Step:    1  2  3  4
Pitch:   0  +3 +6 +9

This sounds like the drum is getting higher with each step—musical and interesting.

Simulating Different Tom Pitches

Most drum kits have three toms: low, mid, and high. You can simulate this with one sample:

Low tom:  pitch = -6
Mid tom:  pitch = 0
High tom: pitch = +6

Adding Texture Within a Track

Vary pitch subtly to prevent monotony:

Step:    1  2  3  4   5  6  7  8
Pitch:   0 -1 +1 -1  +2 +1 -2 0

These small pitch shifts keep the sound interesting without being obvious.

Pro Tips

Combine with Velocity: Low pitch values often sound better with lower velocity. Higher pitches can handle higher velocity without sounding harsh.

Use Sparingly: Extreme pitch shifts (like -12 or +12) are dramatic. For subtle musicality, try using -3 to +3 instead.

Descending Fills: A classic technique is to descend in pitch over a fill: +12, +8, +4, 0. This creates a satisfying downward motion.

Lock It: You can lock the pitch for an entire track so all steps use the same pitch value, then adjust individual steps as needed.

Pitch + Decay: Combine pitch shifts with shorter decay times for crisp, pitch-bent hits that sound like percussion fills on a classic drum machine.

Velocity (VEL)

What is Velocity?

Velocity controls how hard each drum sound is played. Think of it like the force with which someone hits a drum with a drumstick.

In real drumming:

  • Light touch = quiet, subtle sound
  • Medium hit = normal playing volume
  • Hard whack = loud, punchy sound

On the Groovebox, velocity works the same way. You control how hard each step plays from 0 (silent) to 100 (maximum loudness).

Why Does Velocity Matter?

If every hit on the same drum plays at exactly the same volume, it sounds robotic and unnatural. Real drummers vary how hard they hit—they might hit the snare softly during a verse and crack it hard during a chorus.

By adjusting velocity on individual steps, you add human feel and musical expression to your patterns.

Velocity Ranges

Velocity Value What You Hear When to Use
0–51 Soft, quiet sound Ghost notes, subtle accents, quiet sections
52–153 Medium, normal playing level Standard groove, typical beats
154–204 Strong, punchy sound Accents, emphasis, drop beats
205–255 Loud, maximum impact Kicks and crashes, climactic moments

Example: Building Dynamics

Here's a snare track that sounds more natural because velocity varies:

Step:    1  2  3  4
Velocity: 80 100 75 90

Compare this to a robotic version where every hit is identical:

Step:    1  2  3  4
Velocity: 100 100 100 100

The first pattern sounds like a real drummer playing with feel. The second sounds mechanical.

How to Adjust Velocity

  1. Select the track you want to edit on the main screen
  2. Short Press the encoder to enter track-edit mode
  3. Rotate the encoder until you are on the step you want to edit
  4. Medium Press the encoder to enter Step Edit mode
  5. Rotate the encoder to cycle through the popup items until you see "VELOCITY"
  6. Short Press encoder (cursor moves to the value field)
  7. Rotate the encoder to change the velocity value (0–255)
  8. Lower values = softer sound. Higher values = harder/louder sound
  9. Short press encoder to accept selected value
  10. Short press KEY0 to exit step edit mode and return to track-edit mode

Using the Edit Popup

When you press medium press, a popup appears showing all editable parameters for the selected step:

Edit Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        +00
   >DECAY<        50%
    PROBABILITY  100%
    MUTE   OFF
    CHAIN   NO
  1. Navigate between parameters: Rotate the encoder to scroll through Velocity, Pitch, Decay, Probability, Mute, and Chain. The highlighted parameter is between `>..<`.
  2. Edit a parameter: When a parameter is highlighted, press the encoder (short press) to enter edit mode
  3. Change the value: Rotate the encoder to increase or decrease the value: 
    Edit Step 5
    VELOCITY     >100<
    PITCH        +00
    DECAY        50%
    PROBABILITY  100%
    MUTE   OFF
    CHAIN   NO
  4. Confirm the value: Press the encoder (short press) to lock in the new value and return to parameter selection
  5. Continue editing: Repeat steps 1-4 to adjust other parameters
  6. Close the popup: Press KEY0 (short press) to close the popup. Your changes are saved.

Quick Reference

Parameter Range What It Does
VEL (Velocity) 0–255 How hard the step plays (0=silent, 255=maximum)
PITCH -24 to +24 Pitch offset in semitones
DECAY 10–100% How long the sound rings out
PROB 0–100% Probability (chance) the step plays
MUTE ON / OFF Silence this step
CHAIN See Chapter 11 Pattern chaining settings

Pro Tips

Create Swing: Make even-numbered steps slightly quieter than odd-numbered steps to add groove:

Step:    1  2  3  4
Velocity: 90 70 90 70

Add Emphasis: Make specific important steps louder to emphasize them. This draws the listener's ear to key moments in your pattern.

Ghost Notes: Set some steps to very low velocity (10–20) for subtle "ghost notes" that add texture without overwhelming the mix.

Match Your Drums: Different drum samples respond differently to velocity. Punch drums (kicks, snares) benefit from varied velocity. Metallic sounds (cymbals, hi-hats) can work with more consistent velocity.

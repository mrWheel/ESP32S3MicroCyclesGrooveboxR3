Decay (DECAY)

What is Decay?

Decay controls how long a sound lasts after you trigger it. It determines whether the sound stops quickly or fades away gradually.

Think of real-world sounds:

  • A kick drum has a short decay—it hits and quickly stops
  • A cymbal crash has a long decay—it rings out for several seconds
  • A snare crack has medium decay—quick attack, then fades

On the Groovebox, you control the decay from 0 (cuts off almost instantly) to 100 (rings out for a long time).

Why Does Decay Matter?

Different drum sounds have different natural decay characteristics. By adjusting decay:

  • Shape your tone: Short decay = punchy and tight. Long decay = washy and atmospheric
  • Control mix clarity: Shorter decays prevent sounds from overlapping and muddying the mix
  • Create effects: Extremely short decays sound like clicks. Very long decays create pad-like atmospherics
  • Match the music: Fast songs need tighter decay. Slower songs can use longer decay for more space

Understanding Decay Values

Decay ranges from 10% (very short) to 100% (very long). Values below 10% are not available to protect against excessively clipped sounds.

Decay Value What You Hear Use Case
10–25% Short and punchy Kicks, snares, tight drums, percussive hits
26–50% Medium, natural length Standard drum sounds, typical cymbals
51–75% Long, ringing sound Open hats, crashes, ambient pads
76–100% Very long, sustained tail Atmospheric effects, ambient cymbal rings, sustained tones

How to Adjust Decay

  1. Select the track you want to edit
  2. Short Press the encoder to enter track-edit mode
  3. Rotate the encoder until you are on the step you want to edit
  4. Medium Press the encoder to enter Step Edit mode
  5. Rotate the encoder to cycle through the popup items until you see "DECAY"
  6. Short Press encoder (cursor moves to the value field)
  7. Rotate the encoder to change the decay value (0–100)
  8. Lower values = shorter decay. Higher values = longer decay
  9. Short press encoder to accept selected value
  10. Short press KEY0 to exit step edit mode and return to track-edit mode

Practical Examples

Building a Complete Drum Kit from One Sample

Here's how you can shape one sound into different drum-like sounds using decay and other parameters:

Desired Sound Decay Value Why
Tight Kick 20–30 Punchy, cuts off quickly
Snare 30–45 Natural snare ring time
Hi-Hat (Closed) 10–20 Very short and tight
Hi-Hat (Open) 60–100 Rings out like a real open hat
Cymbal Crash 100 (maximum) Long, shimmering tail

Creating Variation in a Pattern

Use decay changes to add interest without changing the actual sound:

Step:  1  2  3  4   5  6  7  8
Decay: 50 40 50 35  45 60 40 50

This creates a living, breathing groove even though the drum is the same sample.

Pro Tips

Match Your Genre: Trap and hip-hop often use short decay for a snappy feel. House and techno might use slightly longer decay for a smoother flow.

Prevent Muddiness: If multiple sounds are overlapping and the mix sounds cloudy, try shortening the decay of background sounds.

Create Fills: Build interest in a fill by gradually lengthening decay towards the end, creating a sense of expansion and release.

Stack Sounds: Use a very short decay (10) on one layer and a longer decay (60) on another layer of the same sound for extra dimension.

Experiment: Try extreme values on different sounds. A 0-10 decay on a cymbal becomes a click. A 100 decay on a kick becomes ambient.

Using the Step Popup

When you press medium press, a popup appears showing all editable parameters for the selected step:

Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        +00
   >DECAY<        50%
    PROBABILITY  100%
    STEP   ON
    CHAIN   NO
  1. Navigate between parameters: Rotate the encoder to scroll through Velocity, Pitch, Decay, Probability, Step On/Off, and Chain. The highlighted parameter is between `>..<`.
  2. Edit a parameter: When a parameter is highlighted, press the encoder (short press) to enter edit mode
  3. Change the value: Rotate the encoder to increase or decrease the value: 
    Step 5
    VELOCITY     100
    PITCH        +00
    DECAY        >50%<
    PROBABILITY  100%
    STEP   ON
    CHAIN   NO
  4. Confirm the value: Press the encoder (short press) to lock in the new value and return to parameter selection
  5. Continue editing: Repeat steps 1-4 to adjust other parameters
  6. Close the popup: Press KEY0 (short press) to close the popup. Your changes are saved.
